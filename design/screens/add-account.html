Name an account hearth does not see

Hearth will not see its balance, or what you spend from it. It sees only the money that moves between it and your connected accounts, and keeps those moves out of your spending.

The account's details

What kind of account

The bank's name, or the person's for a private loan. If you connect it later, hearth uses this to offer it back to you.

What the household calls it.

From "SAV ...0417" in the transfers. Helps match them if you connect it later.

A joint account's owners all see it and every move into it, as with a connected one. Nobody else is told it exists.

Proposed from the details above. A name already in use is refused, and the refusal does not say by what.

No balance is asked for. Hearth would have to guess how it has changed since, and a guessed balance is a wrong one.

Design notes

  1. Asked only when there is something to put in it. This page is reached from a group of real transfers, so the member is naming the place money already went, and the button says how many transfers follow it there.
  2. No balance field, on purpose. A balance typed today is wrong tomorrow, because hearth does not see what is spent from this account. Leaving it unknown keeps net worth honest; the foot of the form says why, so its absence does not look like an oversight.
  3. Kinds in the member's words. "Money owed to you" and "Loan" decide Assets or Liabilities without the member ever choosing a ledger root.
  4. The last digits come from the evidence. "SAV ...0417" is in the transfers' own description, so hearth fills it in, and later uses it to offer this account when Bramblewood is connected.
  5. Ownership works as it does for a connected account. Joint with someone means they see it; nobody else learns it exists. The ledger name refusal is the household's one accepted probe, and says nothing about what it hit.
  6. The preview shows the honest cost: "Not known" in the balance column, and the net worth figure saying what it leaves out with a dashed chip, the design's "not there yet".
