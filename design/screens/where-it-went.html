Design notes

  1. One question, asked of the evidence. Each group is rows that went to the same place, so "where did it go?" is answered once for 22 transfers, not 22 times. The page never asks the member to list their accounts from memory.
  2. The answers are kinds of place, not ledger names. "An account of mine", "someone else", "someone in this household". Each says in its second line what it does to spending, because that is what people worry a transfer will do.
  3. The unseen side is drawn dashed. The two-sided strip shows the account hearth sees as a solid box and the other side as a dashed one: grey and dashed is the design's "not there yet", never the violet of "withheld".
  4. Matched moves are counted, not listed. 21 transfers between connected accounts need nothing from anyone, so they are one line in the summary.
  5. A settled group shrinks to one line with its Undo, so the page reads as progress and a wrong answer is one click from undone.
  6. A tie is a question, not a guess. When two rows match equally well, both are shown with their dates, and only between the member's own accounts. A row on another member's account is never offered, not even as a count.
  7. The loan says what it cannot do. Interest is inside each payment and hearth cannot split it yet; the page says so beside the button rather than in a help page.
  8. On a phone the two sides stack, the arrow turns down, and each answer is a full-width card.
