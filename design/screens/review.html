Design notes

  1. Grouped by the decision it needs, not by date. One click settles a group whose members are alike; the unsure ones are open by default because each needs its own look.
  2. The reason is shown, not a confidence score. "Like 2 past rulings" or "New merchant" tells you what to check. "62%" tells you nothing you can act on.
  3. The total at stake is always next to the count. Eight subscriptions worth $96 are a different decision from four transfers worth $1,840.
  4. Accept-all is secondary and states its scope. It is a deliberate bulk decision, so it says exactly how many it books and which it leaves open, and it is not the loudest button on the page.
  5. Auto-accept is gone. Decision 5 takes it out of the MVP. The reminder stays, with a sentence saying nothing books itself.
  6. Accept stays disabled until a category is chosen for the rows with nothing to suggest. On a phone each row stacks: merchant and amount, then the category and the button.
