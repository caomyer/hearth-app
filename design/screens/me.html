Design notes

  1. Every section says who else can see it. "Household sees" or "Only you" sits on each panel, so the owner never has to guess what their partner is looking at. The sentence under the title says it once in words.
  2. The chart answers "am I on track this month?" Cumulative spend against a typical month and its usual range, so a slow start or a heavy weekend reads at a glance. A bar per day would show noise, not pace.
  3. Review is an action, not a statistic. The queue is broken down by the kind of decision it needs, with the one button that starts it.
  4. Signs, not just colour. Money in carries a "+" and green; money out carries a true minus sign and stays in ink. Most rows are spending, so painting them all red would make red meaningless.
  5. Balances keep cents; the hero does not. The account table reconciles to the cent against statements, and its total ($611,295.59) is the hero ($611,295) at a different precision. Whole-dollar figures are rounded so the household parts add up exactly.
  6. Grouped by day, with the day's spending. Dates are row-group headers rather than a repeated column, which frees the width a phone needs for the payee and the amount.
