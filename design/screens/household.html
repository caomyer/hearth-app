Design notes

  1. One hero. Household net worth is the only number at display size. It is the figure nobody can get from their own bank app, so it earns the top of the household page.
  2. Whole dollars for aggregates, cents for line items. Category amounts keep cents because they reconcile against transactions; the hero and the member totals do not. Whole-dollar parts are rounded so they add up exactly to the whole-dollar total.
  3. Composition, not a pie. A single 10px bar with direct labels says who holds what. Each owner keeps the same colour on every screen.
  4. Charts carry their finding as a sentence. "Dining is $196 above where it usually is" is the answer; the bars are the evidence. Every chart has a table twin.
  5. Privacy is visible inside the data. Sam shares a monthly total but not categories, so Sam's spending appears as a hatched row with its total and the reason, instead of being silently left out or silently folded in.
  6. Unreviewed money is disclosed. The category totals include 19 transactions nobody has checked yet, and the footer says so.
  7. Spending and income are shared separately. The approved mock shows household income, so "Monthly income total" is its own switch beside "Monthly spending total". Income is off by default (firstmate, 2026-10-18), so until a member turns it on, household income and the savings rate read "without" them. No switch covers more than its label says. See rationale.md.
  8. Paid days and typical-by-date ticks are transactions detail. "Fixed: Housing, paid Oct 1" and the typical-by-the-18th ticks appear here because everyone in these categories shares transactions with the viewer. A single payment's day and amount is an individual transaction, so a viewer who sees only someone's categories gets the category amount only: Housing as an ordinary row, no ticks, no "over typical" note. See rationale.md.
