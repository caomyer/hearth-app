Design notes

  1. The total is shown as a sum you can check. "How it adds up" puts the parts next to the hero, to the cent, so the household figure is never a black box. Sam's part appears as a single shared number, marked as such.
  2. The chart answers the question people actually ask: are we getting richer because we save, or because the market went up? A plain net worth line cannot tell you that. Saved sits on the baseline; market moves stack on it or hang below zero.
  3. Sharing narrows the chart, and the chart says so. Splitting Sam's change into saving and market would reveal more than Sam's total, so the chart covers what you can see and states the boundary in its legend.
  4. Every price carries its time; the footer names its source. Decision 6. "Fri close" on a Sunday is correct, not stale, so the stale state (see the states gallery) is reserved for a price older than the last close.
  5. Recompute is explicit. Net worth recomputes daily and on request. The button is small because it is rarely needed; the as-of line is always present because it always matters.
  6. Units keep three decimals, prices two, values cents. On a phone the units column goes; value and price stay, so nothing needed to check a position is lost.
