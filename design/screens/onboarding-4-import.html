Design notes

  1. Progress says what is happening and how much is left, in counts people can sanity-check, not a spinner. Each step's result stays on screen when it finishes.
  2. Balances are a visible step. Every account opens at the balance its bank reports, and the books are checked against it; this is the moment a new member learns why they should trust the numbers.
  3. The handover is honest about scale. Two years is over a thousand transactions. The page says so, and shows why it is not as bad as it sounds: grouped, the largest eight groups are most of it.
  4. Reviewing can start before the import ends. Waiting is the worst part of onboarding; there is no reason to make anyone watch a bar. This mock shows the import finished, the moment the one-sided rows are known.
  5. Sharing is offered here, as the quiet second action, because this is the first moment the member has data the household could see.
  6. Where money went is asked here, not earlier, and not as a step of its own. Before the import hearth has nothing to show, so "which other accounts do you have?" would be a questionnaire answered from memory. After it, hearth asks about the actual transfers, grouped, which takes seconds. A member with nothing one-sided never sees the panel, which is why it is part of this step rather than a fifth one that is often empty.
  7. It comes before the review, as the primary action. Naming the other side first turns 62 rows into 5 answers and teaches every later transfer where it goes, so the review that follows is smaller. "Connect another bank" sits beside it for the member who realises they would rather connect that bank now.
  8. Matched moves are a step result, not a task. Transfers between connected accounts, and card payments, are counted once by hearth itself; the step says so in a count.
