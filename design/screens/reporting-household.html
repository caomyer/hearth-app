Design notes

  1. The month that just ended leads, not the one that just began. On the real Household page on Nov 1, every spending figure read "Nov 1–1" and the whole lower half said "History incomplete". A household reads its money the way it reads a statement: last month, in full. The month in progress is the small panel, and it does not compare itself with typical before the 8th, because one $53 purchase on the 1st is not "$34 over typical".
  2. Net worth stays the hero; the month answer is the first panel under it. The captain kept household net worth as the one figure at display size (decision 2, declined). "Left over in October" sits directly beneath it, so "are we saving anything?" is answered high on the page without a display-size figure that, under default sharing, usually reads "without" someone.
  3. In, out and left over add up, over the same parts. Left over needs income, and Jordan keeps income private, so all three figures cover Alex, Sam and the joint accounts and say "without Jordan" once. Jordan's spending total still appears where it may: as one hatched row under "Where October went".
  4. A full month compares with full months. October against the middle of the six months before is a category a month, which the category facet already shares, so Sam's categories take part. Typical-by-this-date, which reads off when money went out, still needs transactions, exactly as today.
  5. Every sentence is a door. Each category row, each change and each month in the chart opens Explore with that question already asked. A finding is the start of a question, not the end of one.
  6. What changed names places only where the viewer sees transactions. Ridgeline Auto and Lantern Noodle Bar are Alex's own rows. Sam's part changes by category alone, and the panel says so in the same words whether or not Sam's spending moved.
  7. Coming up is only accounts the viewer can see into. A bill's day and amount is a transaction. The footer says whose bills these are, in words that do not depend on whether anyone else has bills.
