Design notes

  1. Learned the hard way, designed in. The existing setup once filed two joint accounts under each other's names and nothing caught it. So every mapping needs its own confirmation, and the button says how many are left.
  2. The near-name warning names both accounts and says what to check. Balances from the bank sit beside each mapping, so a swap shows up as a number that does not belong.
  3. Ownership is chosen per account, with every combination of members. Decision 4 makes ownership many-to-many; the control offers joint ownership with any set of members, not a single "shared" flag.
  4. Ledger names are shown in full, in monospace. They are what the ledger will contain, and one word is the difference that matters here.
  5. On a phone each mapping stacks: the bank account, then where it goes, then the confirmation, so nothing is squeezed into a row.
