Design notes

  1. A question is a sentence with blanks. Show what for whom by what over when. Each blank is a short fixed list, in household words, so someone who has never kept a ledger can change one word and get a new answer. It is a GET form: the address is the question, so Back works and a view can be bookmarked or sent to the other member, who sees it at their own grain.
  2. Starter questions for a first visit. The four links under the sentence are the top-ranked household questions in reporting.md, so the page is never a blank query box.
  3. Every figure opens the next question. A month in the chart narrows to that month; a row narrows to that category; a cell narrows to both. In the mock only the Dining path is wired; follow it with the step chips at the top.
  4. Who is in this view, always. The panel names every part of the household and the grain it is in at, in words that depend only on what each person shares, never on the data. Jordan shares a spending total, so Jordan is a full segment in a chart by who, and one hatched row in a table by category.
  5. The table is fava's monthly changes, made readable. Rows are what you group by, columns are months, with a typical column and a total. Shading is per row, so a category's own big month stands out (Gifts in December, Travel in July) without the rent drowning everything else.
  6. No free query. There is no text box that takes a filter expression, and no way to name an account, a person or a date range the lists do not offer. Why: reporting.md, Disclosure.
